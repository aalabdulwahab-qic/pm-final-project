Question 2 · Size the difference

On average, how many points higher is Month 1 retention for the Intent + badges cohorts than for the Exact-name cohorts?

Working: Exact-name: (64 + 62 + 60) ÷ 3 = 62%. Intent + badges: (73 + 76 + 78) ÷ 3 ≈ 76%. Gap ≈ 14 points.

Look again: even for the best cohort, 22 points are lost between Month 0 and Month 1 (27 for Dec, 22 for Feb). That is still the largest drop in every row. The first visit is where users leave.

Reflect: the launch coincided with the lift, but that is correlation. What else changed in Dec–Feb that could explain it?

Question 3 · Find the opportunity

Which segment generates the biggest share of all data requests?

Working (per 100 users): Decision-makers 22 × 0.4 ≈ 9. Analysts 37 × 1.1 ≈ 41. Viewers 41 × 2.3 ≈ 94. Total ≈ 144, so Viewers ≈ 66%, Analysts ≈ 28%, Decision-makers ≈ 6%.

The heaviest platform users (Decision-makers, 4.8 sessions/week) are the smallest source of requests. The lightest users make the most requests, work from the most local copies, and respond most to trust signals.

Reflect: why might the group with the most local copies respond most to a trust signal?

    Final · Your recommendation (unlocks after all three questions)

    Use your evidence board to answer leadership.

    Best-supported answer: Improve Trust for Occasional Viewers.

    Why: The biggest relative loss is after trust info is checked (61%). Viewers drive about 66% of requests, hold the most local copies, and show the largest response to trust signals (▼ 22 pts). Search is not the main problem, since 78% already see results.

    A good first test: Show a certified/owner/freshness summary on the asset page for Viewers only, and compare "used in briefing" rates against a control group.

    Still unknown: Why people leave after checking trust info (too few certified assets? unclear definitions?). Understand could be the real cause. Your test should separate the two.
